Do government deficits travel with trade deficits?

The twin deficits hypothesis says that when a government borrows more, the country's current account worsens too. If it holds, the current account should have a positive slope on the fiscal balance.

This page tests that for the United States, Germany and Japan using IMF and BEA annual data (% of GDP, through 2024). Hover any chart to read exact values.

5. What the evidence says

Weak and mixed overall

Slopes are positive for Germany and Japan, but zero or negative for the U.S. in every specification.

Germany: levels only

Germany's strong levels slope (about 0.97) disappears in first differences. Both series trend over 2000–2018, which inflates the levels result.

Japan: small but real

Japan is the only country with a significant first-difference slope (0.11, 2001–2024), and it fades when COVID years are dropped.

Levels may be spurious

Unit-root tests mostly fail to reject, and no cointegration test has a p-value below 0.11, so first differences are the safer test.

Limitations

  • Small samples: 24 to 45 annual observations. Newey-West p-values are approximate and unit-root tests have low power.
  • Mixed U.S. source: the U.S. fiscal series is BEA (total government), while Germany and Japan use IMF WEO. The two U.S. series correlate at 0.986 over 2001–2024.
  • Bivariate only: no controls for private saving, investment or the real exchange rate, and no cyclical adjustment.
  • Correlation is not causation. Neither the scatter nor the rolling correlation identifies the direction of effect.
